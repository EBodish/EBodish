My GIS Portfolio

A final collection of my strongest GIS work, showing how maps can be used to analyze housing patterns, vacancy, and environmental risk.

About This Portfolio

Throughout this GIS course, I learned how to use spatial data to investigate real-world problems and communicate patterns through maps. This portfolio highlights three of my strongest projects from the semester: a percent occupied housing map, a percent vacant housing map, and an environmental risk map of Flint, Michigan. Together, these maps show how GIS can be used to analyze housing conditions, neighborhood change, infrastructure risk, and environmental vulnerability. These projects also demonstrate my ability to organize spatial data, symbolize patterns clearly, and use maps to tell a meaningful story.

Map 1: Percent Occupied Housing

Map Narrative

This map shows the percentage of occupied housing units across the study area. The problem being explored is how housing occupancy varies across different neighborhoods and what those patterns may suggest about residential stability. Areas with high occupancy may indicate stronger housing demand, more stable residential communities, or fewer unused housing units. By mapping percent occupied housing, it becomes easier to identify where people are actively living and where housing is being used most consistently. This map helps tell a story about the spatial distribution of housing activity and how GIS can reveal patterns that may not be obvious in a table.

GIS Tools and Skills Used

  • Using census or housing data in a GIS framework
  • Calculating percentages from attribute data
  • Creating a choropleth map
  • Choosing a color scheme to show variation across space
  • Designing a clean final layout with a title, legend, scale bar, and north arrow

Map 2: Percent Vacant Housing

Map Narrative

This map displays the percentage of vacant housing units across the study area. The problem being addressed is identifying where housing vacancy is most concentrated and what that may suggest about neighborhood conditions. Higher vacancy rates can point to possible issues such as disinvestment, population loss, affordability problems, or uneven development. By mapping vacancy as a percentage, the map makes it easier to compare neighborhoods of different sizes and understand where vacant housing is more common. This project shows how GIS can be used to turn housing data into a visual story about neighborhood change and potential areas of concern.

GIS Tools and Skills Used

  • Working with housing vacancy data
  • Calculating percent vacant housing from attribute fields
  • Creating a choropleth map using graduated colors
  • Comparing spatial patterns between occupied and vacant housing
  • Using cartographic design to make the map readable and visually clear

Map 3: Environmental Risk in Flint, Michigan

Map Narrative

This map examines environmental risk in Flint, Michigan by showing how water infrastructure and pollution-related data overlap across space. The problem being explored is who may be most at risk for exposure to unsafe drinking water or environmental hazards. By combining service line data, ward boundaries, rivers, and toxic release information, the map helps show how risk can be connected to both infrastructure and location. The map does not solve the Flint water crisis by itself, but it helps identify areas where environmental vulnerability may be more concentrated. This project demonstrates how GIS can be used to support environmental justice analysis and communicate public health concerns visually.

GIS Tools and Skills Used

  • Layering multiple environmental and infrastructure datasets
  • Using buffers to analyze proximity to rivers or pollution sources
  • Joining tabular data to spatial data
  • Clipping layers to the Flint study area
  • Creating a final map layout that communicates environmental risk clearly
